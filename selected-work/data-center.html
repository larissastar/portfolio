Data Center Supply Chain: Part Management

This project focused on improving key supply chain workflows for data center operations, with a strong emphasis on clarity, speed, and usability. It addressed critical tasks such as looking up parts and their alternates, and enabling dock workers to create new Oracle part numbers for incoming shipments.

With a fast turnaround, the work required close collaboration with another designer and strong alignment across product, engineering, and stakeholders. Through user interviews and regular reviews, we iterated quickly to deliver end-to-end workflows that balanced speed with usability.

ROLE & RESPONSIBILITIES
  • Design Manager
  • Primary Designer, collaborating with another designer due to tight timelines
  • Defined personas, user goals, and conducted user and stakeholder interviews
  • Partnered closely with product managers and engineers to shape direction and ensure feasibility
  • Conducted user reviews and collected unmoderated feedback, iterating designs based on insights
  • Balanced speed of delivery with maintaining clear and usable experiences
Users and User Goals

We identified two key personas: Dock Workers responsible for receiving shipments, who needed to create temporary part numbers for unlabeled items, and the Hardware Order Management team, who needed to look up parts and their alternates when ordering for data center buildouts.

Part Lookup Screens

I created a comprehensive set of screens covering part lookup workflows, enabling users to quickly search, filter, and locate hardware components for their data center needs.

Create New Part and Supply Requests Screens

These screens support the creation of new hardware parts and supply requests, allowing users to manually enter individual parts or create batches by uploading Excel spreadsheets.

Submitted Requests Screens

These screens display submitted supply requests, allowing users to track status, review details, and manage pending or completed orders.

KEY INSIGHTS

The end-to-end workflows for receiving shipments and ordering hardware were successfully designed and well received by stakeholders. The solution addressed critical operational needs while remaining clear and efficient for users in high‑pressure environments.

This project highlighted the value of rapid collaboration, continuous user feedback, and focused design decisions in delivering impactful outcomes within tight timelines.
